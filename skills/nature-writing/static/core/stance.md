# Default stance (writing)

## Author evidence comes first

- Do not invent results, mechanisms, references, methods, novelty, sample sizes, statistics, uncertainty, or limitations.
- Write the argument before writing the sentences.
- Distinguish a missing **scientific premise** from a missing stylistic preference. Only the former should block confident drafting.
- When information is absent but a useful scaffold can still be produced, use explicit placeholders/assumptions instead of silently filling the gap.

## Reader workflow

See `../../../nature-shared/core/reader-workflow.md` (loaded via manifest `always_load`) for the reader-question sequence. Draft and revise so the manuscript makes those answers recoverable, but do not force one section/paragraph structure simply to mirror the sequence.

## Claim discipline

- Use ambitious but bounded claims.
- Classify the claim before choosing the verb: observed, estimated, associated, causally identified, inferred, simulated, proved, hypothesized, enabled, or interpreted.
- Calibrate verbs such as `show`, `demonstrate`, `suggest`, `indicate`, `enable`, `may`, and `could` to the actual warrant.
- Remove unsupported novelty and universal claims (`first ever`, `unprecedented`, `revolutionary`) unless the evidence genuinely supports the stated scope.
- Do not hide an incremental, replication, validation, negative, null, resource, or synthesis contribution merely to fit a discovery narrative.

## Intake — build a writing contract before drafting

Identify as far as the supplied material allows:

- **manuscript section / task**: title, abstract, introduction, related-work/literature review, Methods, Results/analysis, Discussion, Conclusion, full outline/manuscript, or initial-submission package;
- **paper / contribution type**: empirical finding, mechanism, method/algorithm/instrument, resource/benchmark, theory/proof, replication/validation, negative/null result, review/synthesis, practical/clinical/policy implication, or interpretive argument;
- **research paradigm / evidence type**: experimental, observational, clinical, computational, theoretical, qualitative, mixed-method, historical/source-based, review, etc.;
- **central question/tension**;
- **answer / contribution**;
- **evidence chain**: figures, measurements, comparisons, datasets, statistics, proofs, cases, sources, themes, or analyses;
- **boundary**: where the claim stops or what remains uncertain;
- **intended reader / field**;
- **target journal/content type/word limit** if provided;
- **terminology**: recurring methods, models, datasets, constructs, populations, metrics, abbreviations and notation. Record canonical forms in the Terminology Ledger (`../../../nature-shared/core/terminology-ledger.md`).

## Alignment gate — use only for high-leverage ambiguity

Do **not** interrupt normal drafting merely because several stylistic choices are possible.

Use the alignment gate from `workflow.md` only when a wrong assumption would materially change one of these:

- the central claim or research question;
- causal versus associational interpretation;
- study design/evidence class;
- contribution type;
- population/system/scope boundary;
- target article type where exact compliance matters.

When that happens, surface the proposed argument and the few high-leverage assumptions. If clarification is unavailable or immediate drafting is preferred, produce the safest useful scaffold with explicit placeholders rather than inventing evidence.

Missing wording preferences, paragraph count, transition style, or prestige tone are **not** reasons to stop.
