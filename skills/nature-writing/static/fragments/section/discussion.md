# Section: Discussion (writing)

## Reader job

Discussion answers:

`what do the findings mean -> how do they relate to alternatives/prior knowledge -> what remains uncertain -> where can the conclusion travel -> why does it matter`

Do not require a single linear `advance -> meaning -> prior work -> limitations -> future` sequence. Published Discussions often revisit these moves recursively for each major finding.

## Finding-centered cycle

For each major finding, select only the needed moves:

1. restate the finding at the level required for interpretation;
2. interpret mechanism/meaning;
3. compare with prior studies/theory;
4. explain agreement/disagreement;
5. consider alternative explanations;
6. state implication;
7. qualify by design, uncertainty, population, model, measurement, or scope.

Then integrate the cycles into the paper-level answer.

## Paper-level moves

After or between finding cycles, the Discussion may:

- integrate several results into one explanation;
- distinguish what changed from what remains uncertain;
- discuss generalizability/external validity;
- identify strengths that affect confidence;
- identify limitations and their **consequence for interpretation**;
- state practical/clinical/theoretical implications proportional to evidence;
- identify future work that follows from a specific unresolved question.

## Qualification placement

Do not hide all boundaries in a generic final limitations paragraph.

Put important qualifications near the claims they constrain, then summarize major paper-level limitations if the genre expects a dedicated section.

A limitation is useful only when its consequence is clear:

`limitation -> which inference it weakens/does not weaken -> what evidence would resolve it`

## Relation to prior work

Do more than say a result is `consistent with previous studies`.

Ask:

- consistent in what variable/population/mechanism?
- different under what condition?
- does the present evidence resolve a prior contradiction?
- does it narrow or extend a previous claim?
- is disagreement methodological, contextual, statistical, conceptual, or genuinely substantive?

## Stance

Separate:

- directly observed result;
- interpretation consistent with the result;
- proposed mechanism;
- broader generalization;
- recommendation.

Each step may require a different degree of certainty.

Use `may`, `could`, `suggest`, or `is consistent with` when the inference is indirect. Do not hedge a directly measured result merely because it appears in Discussion, and do not promote association into causation because the sentence sounds more decisive.

## Future work

Future work should arise from a real unresolved dependency:

- distinguish competing mechanisms;
- validate in another population/system;
- test a boundary/failure mode;
- improve measurement;
- establish causality;
- evaluate long-term/practical consequences.

Avoid generic `future studies are needed` or product-style roadmaps unsupported by the evidence.

## Common failure modes

- repeating Results figure by figure;
- comparison with literature without explaining the consequence;
- mechanism claims without discriminating evidence;
- limitations listed as ritual disclaimers;
- using one small result to justify a field-wide conclusion;
- ending with marketing rather than the bounded scientific contribution.

## Handoff to conclusion

The Discussion should leave a clear answer to: **After considering alternatives and limitations, what does this paper still establish?**

That bounded answer is the proper basis for the Conclusion.
