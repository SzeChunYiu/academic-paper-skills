# Profiled target journal — writing

Use this fragment when the user names a journal outside the repository's exact Nature profiles, names a publisher/venue family such as Science/AAAS, Cell Press, IEEE, ACM, PLOS, Springer/BMC, Elsevier, or Wiley, or asks to adapt a manuscript for another journal.

Before drafting submission-sensitive text, read:

1. `../../../nature-shared/journal-formats/journal-resolution.md`
2. `../../../nature-shared/journal-formats/journal-family-profiles.md`

Then resolve `exact journal -> article/content type -> stage -> component`.

## Writing contract

- Use the exact current official journal guide when a title is known and any structural, numeric, template, declaration, citation, or submission requirement matters.
- Treat the family profile as a fallback for tone and workflow questions, not as an exact contract.
- Preserve the manuscript's scientific claim/evidence/boundary while adapting audience assumptions, section architecture, compression, terminology density, and front/back matter.
- Do not make prose sound more causal, novel, general, or certain just to resemble a selective journal.
- Do not force IMRaD, STAR Methods, a Nature summary paragraph, IEEE structure, ACM anonymity, or any other family convention onto an incompatible article type.
- For a journal transfer, first list what is target-independent versus target-dependent. Rebuild only the target-dependent layer.

## Exact-guide lookup checklist

When the target is known, check the official source for the current:

- article/content type
- title and abstract format
- section organization/headings
- length/display/reference constraints if any
- Methods/Supplementary/appendix relationship
- required declarations and availability statements
- anonymization/reviewer-facing requirements
- initial versus revised/accepted formatting

If an exact rule cannot be verified, mark it `unresolved` and draft a scientifically valid generic version instead of inventing a requirement.
