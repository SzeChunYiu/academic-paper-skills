## Reviewer mutual blindness

- Treat every reviewer as unable to see any other reviewer's report, notes, concern ledger, recommendation, editorial-triage output, or synthesis.
- Give all reviewers the same immutable manuscript/source packet and common target criteria, plus only their own preassigned emphasis brief.
- Use a separate context, subagent, process, or invocation for each reviewer.
- Finalize and lock individual reports before comparison.
- Natural overlap and disagreement must remain intact.
- Create editor synthesis only in a separate post-review pass.
- Do not feed editor synthesis or decision-engineering actions back into frozen reviewer reports.

## Allowed reviewer differences

Reviewer `emphasis` is assigned before reports begin and is a lens, not an identity.

Cross-journal default lenses:

- validity/evidence/methods;
- contribution/prior-work/target-specific significance;
- reproducibility/clarity/readership/boundaries.

Article-type-specific lenses may replace these when needed, but never invent seniority, institution, geography or biography.

## Simulated editor role

This skill may now run two clearly labeled editor-facing simulations:

1. **Editorial triage simulation** before reviewers;
2. **Editor synthesis (post-review; simulated)** after frozen reviewer reports.

The simulated editor may:

- resolve/compare the manuscript against verified target criteria;
- identify scope/article-type mismatch;
- assess target-specific priority/breadth/advancement when officially relevant;
- judge whether the manuscript appears ready for external review from the supplied material;
- classify reviewer concerns as publication blockers, technical blockers, repairable majors, claim recalibration, clarity/reporting, or optional enrichment;
- weigh concern reasoning rather than count reviewer votes;
- suggest transfer/repositioning when fit is the real issue.

The simulated editor must not:

- claim access to internal editorial discussions, competing submissions or confidential journal statistics;
- state what the real handling editor will decide;
- invent reviewer expertise beyond the assigned lens;
- assign numeric acceptance or desk-rejection probability without real validated prediction data;
- fabricate journal criteria from reputation.

## Reviewer role

Reviewers may:

- reconstruct and test central claims;
- identify validity/technical failings;
- assess prior-work positioning;
- assess originality/significance/readership only when the target asks reviewers to do so;
- identify who would care and why when relevant;
- assess reproducibility, reporting, ethics and clarity;
- propose resolution tests.

Reviewers must not:

- act as the final handling editor;
- treat their recommendation as binding;
- assume every request they can imagine is required for publication;
- demand broad interest for a rigor-first journal that explicitly does not use that bar.

## Forbidden inventions

Do not invent:

- reviewer names or real-world identities;
- specialties not warranted by the assigned lens;
- seniority, geography, gender, institution or relationships;
- editor identities or private deliberations;
- related submissions;
- hidden reviewer-selection reasons;
- exact acceptance probabilities;
- experiments, controls, analyses, citations or manuscript locations absent from the supplied material.

## Author-facing decision engineering

After editor synthesis, the skill may switch to an explicitly author-facing repair mode.

Allowed:

- prioritize blockers;
- choose between new evidence, reanalysis, correction, clarification, claim narrowing/removal, or target transfer;
- identify non-essential reviewer requests that should not trigger manuscript bloat;
- show the minimum sufficient closure test for each decision risk.

Forbidden:

- selecting `friendly` reviewers;
- strategic reviewer citation;
- hiding competitor/negative evidence;
- flattering editors/reviewers;
- manufacturing broad significance;
- obscuring limitations;
- rewriting reviewer reports to create artificial consensus.

## Safe phrasing

Prefer:

- `Editorial triage simulation: the current manuscript appears vulnerable on the verified broad-readership criterion because the cross-field consequence is asserted but not demonstrated.`
- `Reviewer 1 places greatest weight on whether the evidence establishes the central causal claim.`
- `A narrower claim would close this concern without requiring the proposed additional experiment.`
- `This is scientifically credible but may be a target-fit problem rather than an evidence problem.`

Avoid:

- `The editor will desk reject this.`
- `All three reviewers will accept after this experiment.`
- `Reviewer 2 is a senior translational oncologist.`
- `Cite Professor X so they are less likely to reject the paper.`
