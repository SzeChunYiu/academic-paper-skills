## Publication criteria

For flagship Nature Articles, the exact publication model includes three high-level requirements:

- original scientific research;
- outstanding scientific importance;
- a conclusion of interest to an interdisciplinary readership.

Nature also describes content-type-specific expectations. For example, technical advances and large dataset/resource-like contributions can be considered when they deliver substantial scientific/technical value to the relevant research communities.

These are **Nature-specific editorial criteria**, not universal peer-review standards.

## Editorial triage before peer review

Nature editors make an initial decision about whether to send a submitted Article for external review.

Current public guidance describes this screen in terms including:

- novelty;
- an arresting/illuminating or otherwise compelling result;
- immediate and far-reaching implications;
- broad-reader interest appropriate to Nature.

The initial editorial judgement is distinct from a determination that the work is technically invalid or unimportant to specialists. A manuscript can therefore be scientifically credible yet still be declined at editorial triage for Nature-specific priority/readership reasons.

Operational implication for this skill:

- simulate **editorial triage separately from technical peer review**;
- do not try to repair a true Nature-priority mismatch by simply adding confidence words or cosmetic experiments.

## Readability and broad readership

Nature pays explicit attention to whether highly technical work gives enough background and explains the field-level consequence clearly enough for nonspecialist readers.

For mock triage/review, test whether a competent scientist outside the narrow specialty can recover:

1. the basic problem;
2. what changed;
3. what evidence supports it;
4. why that change matters beyond the immediate technical implementation.

Readability does not permit weakening technical precision or hiding limitations.

## Referee selection and role

Nature's public process describes editors selecting referees for independence and ability to assess the technical aspects fairly, among other practical considerations.

The simulation must not invent identities, institutions, specialties, recent-submission knowledge, or real selection history.

Reviewer emphasis lenses are therefore repo-level analytical devices, not claims about who Nature would actually invite.

## What reviewers are asked to assess

Current Nature peer-review guidance asks reviewers to provide information useful to an editorial decision, including assessment of the manuscript's key results/conclusions and whether the work is technically sound and supported by strong evidence.

Reviewers may also advise on:

- novelty/originality;
- importance/significance;
- relevance to a general Nature audience;
- data and methodology;
- limitations and technical failings;
- clarity/readability.

The exact report format in this repo is an implementation choice, not Nature's mandated referee template.

## How editors use reviewer reports

Nature explicitly separates reviewer recommendations from the final editorial decision.

Operationally:

- editor simulation must **not count accept/reject votes**;
- weigh the reasoning in a concern and the relevance of the assigned review lens;
- a single serious technical criticism can remain decision-critical;
- reviewer opinions about whether a manuscript belongs in Nature are advisory rather than mechanically binding;
- editors remain particularly reluctant to ignore unresolved technical criticisms.

This is why every Major Concern in the skill has a resolution test.

## Decision outcomes and revision

Nature's public peer-review process allows several post-review outcomes, including paths where the manuscript may be accepted or accepted in principle after limited changes, deferred while substantial concerns are addressed, or declined.

For revision simulation, distinguish:

- technical/evidentiary issues that must be closed;
- Nature-specific priority/readership concerns;
- clarity/reporting concerns;
- useful but non-essential reviewer requests.

After claim narrowing or negative new evidence, re-check whether the revised manuscript still meets the Nature-specific importance/breadth case. A technically stronger but materially narrower paper can change target fit.

- apply it to Nature Communications, Nature Machine Intelligence, another Nature Portfolio title, Science, Cell, IEEE, PLOS, JAMA, ACM or any other venue;
- turn the criteria into a numeric acceptance score;
