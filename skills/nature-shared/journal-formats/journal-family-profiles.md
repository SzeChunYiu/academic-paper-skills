# Journal family profiles

> Fallback profiles for journal-aware writing, polishing, citation, and figure work. These profiles deliberately avoid pretending that every journal within a publisher has one format.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-08-19. Exact journal author instructions remain the source of truth; see `journal-resolution.md`.

## How to use this file

Use a family profile only after resolving the exact target as far as possible. Never transfer a numeric limit, mandatory section, citation punctuation rule, template, or figure specification from one journal to another merely because they share a publisher.

For an exact submission, check the target journal's current official author guide and template at use time.

---

## Nature Portfolio

### Typical profile

- Broad-audience clarity matters strongly for flagship and many selective titles, but audience breadth differs across the portfolio.
- Initial submission may be more flexible than accepted/production formatting for some titles.
- Numbered references are common across many Nature Portfolio research titles, but exact styling and limits differ.
- Figure simplicity, clear legends, statistical definitions, data/code availability, and research-integrity checks are prominent.
- Nature Portfolio itself instructs authors to choose the exact journal and follow that journal's own article types, length guidance, figure requirements, and submission details.

### Resolve before applying

- flagship versus specialist title
- article/content type
- exact title/abstract/display/reference limits
- Methods/Supplementary/Extended Data relationship
- data/code/source-data requirements
- initial versus production figure contract

### Exact local profiles

Use repository files for `Nature`, `Nature Communications`, and `Nature Machine Intelligence` when those exact journals are targeted.

### Official starting points

- https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/for-authors/write
- https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide

## Science / AAAS family

### Typical profile

- `Science` and the Science-family journals share a publisher ecosystem but have distinct author instructions and article types.
- Do not infer `Science` flagship length, summary, reference, or supplementary rules for `Science Advances`, `Science Robotics`, `Science Translational Medicine`, or other family titles.
- Manuscript templates and journal-specific author pages should be treated as authoritative for exact mechanics.
- Broad significance and concise framing are often important for selective general-science titles, but field-specific sister journals may assume more specialist background.

### Resolve before applying

- exact Science-family journal
- manuscript type (Research Article, Report, Review, etc.)
- main versus supplementary-material organization
- reference rendering and limits
- figure preparation and revised-manuscript requirements

### Official starting point

- https://cts.sciencemag.org/scc/ (links to current author information for Science-family journals)

## Cell Press

### Typical profile

- Cell Press journals vary materially across life, health, physical, and interdisciplinary science.
- Many life-science research formats use or derive from STAR Methods principles emphasizing structured, reproducible methods and resource reporting.
- Graphical Abstracts, Highlights, In Brief/eTOC-like elements, Key Resources Tables, and STAR Methods are journal/content-type dependent rather than universal submission requirements.
- Do not force life-science STAR structure onto physical-science or interdisciplinary Cell Press titles unless the exact journal requires it.

### Resolve before applying

- exact journal and discipline group
- article type
- whether STAR Methods / Key Resources Table is required
- graphical abstract/highlights/front-matter requirements
- declaration, lead-contact, data/code/materials availability rules
- reference and length rules

### Official starting points

- use the target journal's `Information for Authors` page on cell.com
- Cell Press editorial explanation of STAR Methods: https://crosstalk.cell.com/blog/a-star-upgrade

## IEEE journals and transactions

### Typical profile

- IEEE journal preparation is strongly template-driven.
- The correct template is publication-specific; use IEEE's Template Selector rather than inventing layout from memory.
- Engineering/computing papers commonly need precise problem definition, notation consistency, baseline selection, complexity/runtime or hardware context where relevant, and reproducible implementation/evaluation detail.
- Numbered references are common, but exact publication requirements still come from the target periodical/template.

### Resolve before applying

- exact IEEE journal/transaction/letter
- article category
- publication template and page/overlength rules
- review versus final layout
- mandatory ORCID or author metadata requirements
- graphical abstract/supplementary/data-code options

### Official starting points

- https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/
- https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/authoring-tools-and-templates/tools-for-ieee-authors/ieee-article-templates/

## ACM journals and proceedings

### Typical profile

- ACM uses the `acmart`/Primary Article Template ecosystem, but individual journals, PACM venues, conferences, and tracks can differ in review layout, anonymization, length, and final TAPS workflow.
- Accessibility is a first-class requirement in much of the ACM ecosystem: authors should prepare accessible documents and useful figure descriptions/alt text when required by the venue.
- Do not assume all ACM venues use the same one-column/two-column review format or the same anonymity model.

### Resolve before applying

- exact ACM journal/conference/track
- review format and anonymity policy
- length policy
- `acmart` class options or Word workflow
- TAPS requirements for accepted material
- CCS concepts/keywords and accessibility requirements

### Official starting point

- https://www.acm.org/publications/authors/submissions

## PLOS journals

### Typical profile

- PLOS journals have strong open-science, data-availability, ethics, and reporting-policy expectations.
- PLOS ONE currently uses Vancouver-style numbered references and has its own manuscript organization and figure-upload workflow; do not assume every detail transfers unchanged to every PLOS title.
- Scientific validity, transparent reporting, and evidence-supported conclusions matter more than mimicking a prestige-journal prose style.

### Resolve before applying

- exact PLOS journal
- article type/study design
- reference style and abstract restrictions
- figure placement/upload rules
- data availability and repository requirements
- reporting checklist or ethics requirements

### Official starting point

- https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/submission-guidelines

## Springer / BMC / SpringerOpen

### Typical profile

- Springer Nature explicitly routes authors to title-specific submission guidelines; BMC/SpringerOpen journals likewise vary in article type and preparation requirements.
- IMRaD is a useful default for empirical research but should not be forced on reviews, protocols, methods, qualitative work, humanities, or journal-specific structures.
- Structured abstracts, declarations, data availability, and reporting guidelines are often title/article-type dependent.

### Resolve before applying

- brand and exact journal
- article type
- structured versus unstructured abstract
- reference style
- declarations and availability statements
- template/file requirements

### Official starting points

- https://support.nature.com/en/support/solutions/articles/6000214274-submitting-a-manuscript-to-a-journal
- target journal's `Submission guidelines` page

## Elsevier journals

### Typical profile

- Elsevier does not impose one universal reference or manuscript format across all journals; the target journal's `Guide for Authors` governs exact requirements.
- Many empirical research articles use familiar title/abstract/keywords/IMRaD-like architecture, but journals vary in article types, structured abstracts, graphical abstracts, highlights, reference style, and template flexibility.
- `Your Paper Your Way` or flexible-format behavior is journal-specific; never assume it applies globally.

### Resolve before applying

- exact journal and article type
- whether initial submission is format-flexible
- abstract/keyword/highlight/graphical-abstract requirements
- reference style and source abbreviation rules
- declarations, CRediT, data-sharing requirements
- artwork/file specifications

### Official starting points

- target journal's `Guide for Authors`
- https://researcheracademy.elsevier.com/writing-research/fundamentals-manuscript-preparation/structuring-article-correctly

## Wiley journals

### Typical profile

- Wiley explicitly instructs authors to follow the specific journal's author guidelines.
- Manuscript structure, reference style, accepted file types, data-sharing expectations, and reporting requirements vary by title and society partner.
- Avoid applying one Wiley house style across all disciplines; many Wiley journals are owned by scholarly societies with their own conventions.

### Resolve before applying

- exact journal/society
- article type
- reference and language style
- file/template rules
- data-sharing policy tier
- figures/tables and supplementary requirements

### Official starting points

- https://authorservices.wiley.com/author-resources/Journal-Authors/Prepare/manuscript-preparation-guidelines.html
- target journal's `Author Guidelines`

## APA-style social and behavioral science journals

This is a **style/reporting family**, not a publisher-wide journal contract.

### Typical profile

- author-date references are common
- explicit construct definition, sampling, statistics/effect sizes/uncertainty, ethical approval/consent where relevant, and transparent limitations are important
- structured reporting depends on study type and journal
- use the exact journal plus current APA style when the target requires APA conventions

Do not treat APA style as evidence that a journal follows every APA reporting or submission rule.

---

## Medical and clinical journals

This is a **discipline/reporting family**.

### Typical profile

- study-design reporting guidelines can be more important than publisher prose style
- registration, ethics, participant flow, outcomes, adverse events, effect sizes/confidence intervals, data sharing, and protocol/statistical-analysis-plan transparency may be required
- Vancouver-like numbered references are common but not universal

Resolve the exact journal and the applicable reporting guideline (for example CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, CARE, TRIPOD, STARD, ARRIVE, or extensions) rather than forcing a generic biomedical template.

---

## Humanities and law journals

These often fall outside IMRaD assumptions.

### Typical profile

- argument/source architecture may dominate over methods/results segmentation
- footnotes/endnotes, notes-bibliography, author-date, Bluebook/OSCOLA, MLA, Chicago, or journal-specific systems may apply
- primary-source editions, archival citations, cases/statutes, and quotations can have discipline-specific rules

Do not rewrite these papers into laboratory-science structure simply because the skill originated in a Nature-oriented repository.

## Cross-family decision table

| Need | What to resolve first | Safe fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Draft a section | discipline + article type + exact journal if known | generic scholarly structure |
| Polish prose | audience + section job + target journal | evidence-preserving academic English |
| Add citations | evidence scope | best-evidence search; style later |
| Format references | exact target journal/style | complete metadata, no invented punctuation |
| Make a figure | conclusion + target journal stage | accessible vector-first scientific figure |
| Final submission audit | exact journal + article type + stage | no claim of compliance until official guide checked |
| Journal transfer | new target + stage | preserve scientific content; rebuild only target-dependent layers |

## Anti-patterns

Never:

- equate journal prestige with evidence quality
- copy Nature title/abstract/legend limits into non-Nature journals
- force STAR Methods into every Cell Press paper
- force IEEE/ACM layout without the correct venue template
- assume an Elsevier/Wiley/Springer publisher implies one reference style
- assume IMRaD for humanities, qualitative, theory, methods, reviews, or commentary formats
- present a family profile as a verified submission requirement
