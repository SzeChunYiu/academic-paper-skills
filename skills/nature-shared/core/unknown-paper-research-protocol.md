# Unknown-paper research protocol

> Fallback contract for academic-paper work when the current skill set does not confidently cover the paper archetype, study design, article type, target venue, reporting norm, writing convention, or figure grammar. Last reviewed: 2026-08-25.

## Principle

When unsure, **research the publication ecology before inventing a rule**.

Do not force the nearest built-in archetype merely to avoid uncertainty.

The goal is to build a temporary, evidence-backed profile for the current manuscript.

## Trigger conditions

Run this protocol when any material question cannot be answered confidently from current loaded guidance, including:

- no existing paper archetype fits without distortion;
- unusual mixed-method or emerging study design;
- unfamiliar discipline/subfield;
- new or niche article type;
- target venue has unusual review or reporting expectations;
- a reviewer requests a convention the session cannot verify;
- uncertainty about what belongs in main text versus Methods/SI;
- uncertainty about what plots/figures normally make a claim evaluable;
- uncertainty about whether a writing move is actually appropriate in this field;
- local corpus guidance conflicts with recent papers;
- target rules may have changed.

## Research order

### 1. Official target sources

If a target is known, inspect current official materials for:

- aims/scope;
- article/content type;
- editorial/peer-review criteria;
- author instructions;
- reporting/ethics/data/code/figure requirements;
- relevant stage: planning, initial submission, revision, production.

Official instructions define compliance, not prose quality.

### 2. Reporting/methodological standards

Identify the applicable reporting ecology when relevant, for example:

- randomized trials;
- observational studies;
- systematic reviews/meta-analyses;
- diagnostic/prognostic studies;
- qualitative research;
- animal studies;
- computational prediction/AI studies;
- datasets/resources;
- software/artifacts;
- domain-specific standards.

Do not invent a checklist. Verify current authoritative guidance.

### 3. Define the comparator profile

Describe the nearest useful papers by:

```text
scientific question/contribution
study design
evidence modality
article type
subfield/audience
target venue/family if useful
publication period
```

Comparability outranks prestige.

### 4. Quick broad profile

Inspect roughly **8–15 recent comparable papers** when available for a fast local profile.

Record descriptive tendencies only:

- whole-paper architecture;
- section naming/order;
- main figure/table count and roles;
- where validation/limitations live;
- Methods/main/SI allocation;
- explanation density;
- recurring statistical/reporting patterns.

Do not convert the median into a mandatory rule.

### 5. Deep near-neighbor reading

Choose **3–6 closest papers** for deep reading.

Annotate:

- problem/tension;
- bounded contribution;
- evidence progression;
- why each evidence block follows the previous one;
- main figure roles and reader questions;
- comparator/control/uncertainty choices;
- claim/boundary calibration;
- explanation depth for unfamiliar concepts;
- paragraph and sentence handoffs;
- what is omitted or delegated to support material.

Learn relations/functions, not wording/layout.

### 6. Counterexample search

Actively find at least one legitimate paper that violates the dominant pattern.

Ask whether the variation is explained by:

- study design;
- evidence type;
- article type;
- audience;
- journal mechanics;
- author-level choice.

A pattern that cannot survive counterexamples should remain a local tendency, not a hard rule.

### 7. Build a temporary archetype profile

Output internally:

```text
Temporary archetype name
Closest built-in archetype(s)
What makes it different
Core reader decision
Required evidence dependencies
Likely section moves
Explanation hotspots
Main figure/table roles
Support-material roles
Reporting/compliance duties
Common local tendencies
Counterexamples
Unresolved uncertainties
Sources + dates checked
```

### 8. Apply conservatively

Use the temporary profile only for the current manuscript unless later validated across independent cases.

## Uncertainty handling

If current web/literature access is unavailable or evidence is weak:

- do not hallucinate a field convention;
- fall back to general scientific validity, reporting transparency, reader logic and claim/evidence alignment;
- label the local convention unresolved;
- avoid exact numeric/format rules unless verified.

## Self-research triggers during iteration

The pipeline should re-enter research when:

- a new reviewer concern depends on an unfamiliar norm;
- a revision changes the paper archetype;
- claim narrowing changes the target audience/venue;
- a new figure type is proposed but its interpretation/standard practice is uncertain;
- a sentence/section writing decision remains disputed after internal reasoning;
- a method/statistical choice needs external methodological support;
- a competing paper/new literature changes novelty or interpretation.

## Writing-specific research

When unsure how to realize a move in prose:

1. define the rhetorical function, e.g. `contrast prior methods`, `explain why analysis B follows A`, `state bounded mechanism claim`;
2. inspect several analogous complete passages from close papers;
3. abstract their information flow, agency, stance and sentence relationships;
4. write original prose from the current manuscript's evidence and author voice;
5. run sentence-logic and surface QA.

Do not copy distinctive phrasing.

## Figure-specific research

When unsure what to show:

1. define the exact claim and reader question;
2. inspect how close papers expose the relevant evidence/uncertainty;
3. identify the scientific role, not just chart type;
4. compare legitimate alternatives;
5. choose from the current data/estimand;
6. reject common plots that obscure the current evidence.

## Decision boundary

Research can add:

- citations;
- context/prior work;
- methodological guidance;
- genre/writing calibration;
- figure/evidence expectations;
- reporting requirements.

Research cannot add a result that the authors did not obtain.

A paper becomes better grounded, not fictional.
