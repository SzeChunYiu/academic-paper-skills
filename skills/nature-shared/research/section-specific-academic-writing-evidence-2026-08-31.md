# Section-specific academic-writing evidence review — 2026-08-31

Purpose: evidence basis for `abstract-information-budget.md` and `manuscript-section-craftsmanship.md`.

This note separates:

- exact/current venue requirements;
- editor/publisher recommendations;
- peer-reviewed genre/readability/meta-research;
- transfer limits.

It does **not** convert one discipline's conventions into universal rules.

## 1. Title and abstract as entry points

### Nature Computational Science — 2025 editorial

Source: *Making titles and abstracts broadly accessible*, Nature Computational Science 5, 437 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s43588-025-00835-4.

Key support:

- title and abstract are primary entry points and may be all many readers see;
- they should be self-contained and accessible enough for intended readership;
- abstract can be treated as a very short paper: brief problem/context, solution/approach, main findings/results, and implications;
- jargon, unnecessary technical terms and inaccessible acronyms reduce reach;
- avoid overselling significance;
- title/abstract should contain relevant discoverable keywords.

Transfer limit: multidisciplinary Nature-family accessibility expectations are stronger than some narrow specialist venues; the functional entry-point principle transfers, exact simplification level does not.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00835-4

### Nature Structural & Molecular Biology — scientific writing editorial

Source: *Scientific writing 101*, Nature Structural & Molecular Biology (2010), DOI 10.1038/nsmb0210-139.

Key support:

- title should highlight the main point;
- abstract should communicate the big picture rather than become bogged down in detail;
- abstract should frame the important question, how it was addressed, and place the result in broader context;
- experimental detail belongs mainly in the body unless a new technique is central;
- end abstract with broader implications when appropriate.

Transfer limit: editorial advice, not a reporting standard.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/nsmb0210-139

### Springer Nature editorial/community guidance on abstract information selection

Source: *How to write an abstract* (Springer Nature Research Communities).

Key support:

- proposes a problem/objective/result/implication sequence;
- explicitly says a few key pieces of data can help but too many numbers are off-putting;
- method detail should be limited unless method is central.

Transfer limit: practical editorial guidance, not peer-reviewed empirical evidence or universal sentence template.

URL: https://communities.springernature.com/posts/how-to-write-an-abstract

### Nature Masterclasses blog summary

Source: *Writing for highly-selective journals*.

Key support:

- abstract should accurately represent the paper;
- include key statistics without alienating readers through too many numbers/acronyms;
- explain what results mean and avoid hype.

Transfer limit: educational/editorial guidance.

URL: https://blogs.nature.com/blog/nature-masterclasses-writing-for-highly-selective-journals/

## 2. Abstract structure and reporting completeness

### CONSORT 2025 explanation and elaboration

Source: CONSORT 2025 explanation/elaboration, BMJ/Lancet/JAMA versions; PMC11995452.

Key support:

- abstracts are often used as screening/decision surfaces and need accurate, sufficiently detailed reporting;
- abstract must not distort the full paper;
- for randomized trials, structured abstracts are recommended and should contain trial design, participant/intervention information, analyzed numbers, primary outcome group results, effect size and precision, harms, registration and funding.

Critical transfer limit:

- this demonstrates why **no universal low-number rule is valid**;
- reporting-intensive clinical abstracts legitimately need multiple numerical objects.

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11995452/

### Structured abstract research

Sources:

- Hartley, *Current findings from research on structured abstracts*, J Med Libr Assoc. 2004;92:368–371, PMCID PMC442180.
- Hartley, *Current findings ... an update*, J Med Libr Assoc. 2014;102:146–148, PMCID PMC4076121.
- Hartley, *Are Structured Abstracts Easier to Read Than Traditional Ones?*, Journal of Research in Reading (1997), DOI 10.1111/1467-9817.00025.

Key support:

- structured abstracts often improve information finding and perceived readability;
- structure can increase information completeness but also length;
- benefit depends on implementation and context.

Transfer limit: strongest evidence is biomedical/psychological journal contexts; do not force headings where venue requires unstructured abstract.

## 3. Numerical density and precision in abstracts

### Barnett 2018 meta-research

Source: Adrian G. Barnett, *Missing the point: are journals using the ideal number of decimal places?*, F1000Research 7:450 (2018), PMID 30135727.

Key support:

- studied >43,000 percentages from ~9,500 abstracts across 23 journals;
- only 55% met the examined presentation guideline;
- using too many decimal places (33%) was more common than too few (12%);
- poor numerical presentation can fatigue readers and reduce readability.

Transfer limit: percentage-specific guidance and medical/general journals; exact ideal decimal rules are context-dependent.

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6073092/

### Cole 2015 on excessive numerical precision

Source: *Too many digits: the presentation of numerical data*, Archives of Disease in Childhood / BMJ-family statistical commentary, PMCID PMC4483789.

Key support:

- too many digits can swamp readers, overcomplicate the story and obscure the message;
- numerical precision should track the scientific quantity rather than software output.

Transfer limit: statistical presentation principles transfer; field-specific significant-digit conventions do not.

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4483789/

### Accessible abstract experiment

Source: Doubleday et al., *Better Writing in Scientific Publications Builds Reader Confidence and Understanding*, Frontiers in Psychology 12:714321 (2021).

Key support:

- experimental comparison of differently written scientific abstracts with 170 readers;
- more accessible versions improved reported readability, understanding and confidence.

Transfer limit: undergraduate-reader experiment across selected topics; does not prescribe one style for specialist articles.

URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.714321/full

## 4. AI-generated abstract failure patterns

### Deng, Li & Gao 2026

Source: *Move patterns, metadiscourse use, and move-metadiscourse connection in ChatGPT-generated and human-written research article abstracts*, Journal of English for Academic Purposes, DOI 10.1016/j.jeap.2026.101731.

Key support:

- ChatGPT and human abstracts showed broadly comparable move inventories but different distributions;
- ChatGPT abstracts used background/context move less often in the studied applied-linguistics corpus;
- metadiscourse distribution differed and AI output showed a more patterned organization.

Transfer limit: applied-linguistics corpus and one generation setup; use as diagnostic evidence, not an AI-detector rule.

### Gao et al. / npj Digital Medicine 2023 comparison

Source: *Comparing scientific abstracts generated by ChatGPT to real abstracts with detectors and blinded human reviewers*, npj Digital Medicine 6:75 (2023).

Key support:

- reviewers found generated abstracts believable and sometimes hard to distinguish;
- generated abstracts were described as vaguer and more formulaic;
- generated content could include fabricated data, highlighting need for source-grounded verification.

Transfer limit: early ChatGPT model and medical abstracts; not a current detector benchmark.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-023-00819-6

### Abstract information-density research

Sources:

- *Diachronic changes in lexical density of research article abstracts*, Lingua 312 (2024), DOI 10.1016/j.lingua.2024.103837.
- *Understanding writing styles of scientific papers in the IS-LS domain: Evidence from abstracts over the past three decades*, Journal of Informetrics 17 (2023), article 101377.

Key support:

- abstracts have become information-dense;
- greater information density can reduce accessibility;
- discipline affects acceptable density and style.

Transfer limit: lexical-density measures are corpus descriptors, not direct abstract-quality scores.

## 5. Introduction

### Nature Computational Science 2025

Source: *On writing accessible computational science papers*, Nature Computational Science 5:515 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s43588-025-00847-0.

Key support:

- introduction should balance broad context and technical detail;
- include relevant background and clear motivation;
- conclude with a concise preview of approach, key findings and implications.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00847-0

### Nature Structural & Molecular Biology 2010

Key support:

- keep introduction concise;
- provide only background required to understand the study and gap;
- do not turn it into a review article;
- end with what the paper shows.

### Cross-disciplinary introduction genre research

Source: Lu et al., *Rhetorical and phraseological features of research article introductions: Variation among five social science disciplines*, System 100 (2021), 102543.

Key support:

- 500 published introductions manually annotated with an adapted CARS model;
- substantial disciplinary variation in move-step distribution and phraseology.

Implication:

- gap/context/present-research functions are useful, but one fixed introduction recipe is not transferable across fields.

## 6. Methods

### Nature Methods 2025

Source: *Reporting methods for reusability*, Nature Methods 22:217 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41592-025-02615-4.

Key support:

- method reporting should support reproducibility/reusability;
- avoid vague shortcut citations such as `used as previously described` when full steps are needed;
- logically organized Methods and subheadings help readers find/reproduce procedures;
- separate code/material availability is useful.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02615-4

### Cotos, Huffman & Link 2017

Source: *A move/step model for methods sections: Demonstrating Rigour and Credibility*, English for Specific Purposes 46:90–106, DOI 10.1016/j.esp.2017.01.001.

Key support:

- corpus of 900 Methods sections across 30 academic fields;
- Methods do rhetorical work beyond procedural chronology, including demonstrating rigour and credibility;
- meaningful cross-disciplinary variation exists.

Implication:

- Methods should be organized around study/design logic and evidential credibility rather than source-code layout or one universal template.

## 7. Results

### Nature Methods 2017

Source: *So you're writing a paper*, Nature Methods 14:1115 (2017), DOI 10.1038/nmeth.4532.

Key support:

- Results and figures are the paper core;
- focus on rationale, observations and direct interpretation;
- provide enough method context to understand the result but keep detailed methods in Methods;
- avoid digressing into extended Discussion.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.4532

### Nature Computational Science 2025

Key support:

- Results should follow the logic of the research rather than execution chronology;
- justify comparison methods and explain practical usefulness;
- statistical significance/uncertainty should be made interpretable when used.

### Results rhetorical-move research

Source: *Macrostructures and rhetorical moves in research articles in nanotechnology*, Frontiers in Psychology (2025), plus literature reviewed therein.

Key support:

- reporting results is central/obligatory in studied corpora;
- commentary and methodological reflection vary with paper purpose;
- common sequences include result followed by comment, but practices vary.

Transfer limit: genre/corpus evidence is descriptive; it should not force identical local commentary across disciplines.

## 8. Discussion

### Nature Structural & Molecular Biology 2010

Key support:

- Discussion should not repeat Results;
- interpret results in broader context;
- explain how they fit or do not fit prior work;
- state conceptual advance and unanswered questions.

### Nature Cancer 2023

Source: *The craft (and art) of scientific writing*, Nature Cancer 4 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s43018-023-00579-y.

Key support:

- Discussion puts findings in perspective;
- connect findings to existing knowledge;
- highlight implications, outstanding questions and future paths;
- closest prior studies can be discussed in more depth here;
- maintain brevity and avoid hype while acknowledging caveats.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-023-00579-y

### Discussion move research

Sources:

- Zuo & Mahfoodh, *Rhetorical organization of Discussion sections...*, Journal of English for Academic Purposes (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.jeap.2026.101720.
- Ash'ari et al., *The rhetorical organization of discussion sections of qualitative research articles...*, Journal of English for Academic Purposes 66 (2023), 101310.

Key support:

- commenting/interpreting results is a central Discussion function;
- move organization is complex and varies across genre/paradigm;
- qualitative and quantitative Discussions share some high-level functions but differ in step realization.

Implication:

- require interpretation/alternative/prior-work/boundary functions when needed, but do not force one move sequence.

## 9. Figures, tables and captions

### Nature Methods 2017 and current formatting guidance

Key support:

- figures are central evidence surfaces;
- legends should decode what is depicted rather than become extended Methods or Discussion;
- exact journal rules may require error-bar/statistical/sample-size information;
- unnecessary detail and display complexity reduce clarity.

### Nature formatting guide

Key support:

- figures should be as simple as compatible with clarity;
- unnecessary panels should be avoided;
- legends begin with a brief title and explain panels/symbols;
- detailed Methods should remain in Methods when a Methods section exists.

Transfer limit: exact legend content differs across Nature titles and other venues.

## 10. Theory/formal work

### NeurIPS checklist/current handbook

Key support:

- abstract and introduction claims must accurately reflect theory/experiments and assumptions/limits;
- theoretical assumptions should be clearly stated or referenced with theorem statements;
- complete proofs can be in main text or supplement, with short intuition/proof sketches encouraged when proofs are moved.

URL: https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist

Implication:

- formal scientific objects, assumptions and proof dependencies must remain reader-visible even under page pressure.

## 11. Exact venue format matters

Examples reviewed 2026-08-31:

### Nature Computational Science

- Article abstract: up to 150 words, unreferenced;
- main text: up to 3,500 words;
- up to six display items;
- Introduction / Results / Discussion / Methods architecture for Articles.

URL: https://www.nature.com/natcomputsci/content

### Nature Methods

- Article abstract: up to 150 words;
- Brief Communication abstract: up to 70 words;
- different main-text/display budgets by type.

URL: https://www.nature.com/nmeth/content

### NeurIPS 2026

- nine content pages including figures/tables;
- references, optional technical appendices and checklist outside content-page count;
- main claims in abstract/introduction must match actual scope and results.

URL: https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook

### JMLR

- formatting guide requires abstract but gives mostly formatting rather than substantive rhetorical prescriptions;
- common-errors page explicitly asks authors to make sure the abstract reads well because it is used to advertise the paper.

URL: https://jmlr.org/format/format.html
URL: https://jmlr.org/format/formatting-errors.html

## 12. Cross-disciplinary transfer rule

The evidence repeatedly shows both stable high-level functions and real disciplinary variation.

Therefore the maintained rule is:

```text
exact venue + article type + reporting standard
+ paper archetype + close peer-reviewed analogues
-> manuscript-specific section functions and information budgets
```

Never derive universal rules such as:

- every abstract has exactly five moves;
- every abstract has at most three numbers;
- Introduction is always three paragraphs;
- Methods always uses the same subheadings;
- Results never contain local interpretation;
- Discussion must contain every possible move;
- Conclusion is mandatory;
- Related Work is mandatory.

The release invariant is functional completeness, reader comprehension and claim/evidence fidelity under the exact publication regime.
